### Introduction

Every great city has a place where its past and present quietly coexist. For Guangzhou, that place is Liwan Lake Park. More than just a scenic spot, this 27-hectare oasis in the heart of the old Xiguan quarter is a living archive of Cantonese civilization, where ancient waterways, imperial gardens, and the collective memory of a city's people converge.

To understand Liwan Lake Park is to understand Guangzhou itself. Its story begins over two thousand years before the first stone was laid for its bridges, when the area was not a park at all but a shimmering bay of the Pearl River, fringed with lychee trees and crisscrossed by fishing boats.

### The Birth of Litchi Bay: Lychees, Diplomats, and Ancient Waters

The history of Liwan Lake Park is inseparable from the history of Litchi Bay (Lizhiwan). According to historical records, the story begins around 196 BC, during the Western Han Dynasty, when the famous official Lu Jia was sent to the Nanyue Kingdom as a diplomat. It is said that Lu Jia planted lychee trees along the bay near his station, and the area was named Litchi Bay because of these trees. This was not merely an act of agriculture; it was a gesture of civilization, bringing the cultivated elegance of the Central Plains to the southern frontier.

Over the centuries, Litchi Bay evolved from a simple agricultural settlement into a center of culture, art, and learning. By the Ming Dynasty, "Liwan Fisherman's Song" (荔湾渔唱) was already listed as one of the Eight Scenic Spots of Guangzhou. The bay had become a place of poetic inspiration, where scholars and artists gathered to compose verses inspired by the shimmering waters and the evening songs of fishermen returning with their catch.

### The Golden Age: Gardens of the Elite

The true flowering of Litchi Bay came during the Qing Dynasty, when the area became home to some of the most celebrated private gardens in southern China. These were not merely gardens in the Western sense but complete cultural universes, where wealthy merchants and retired officials created miniature paradises of rockeries, pavilions, lotus ponds, and poetry.

Among the most famous were Hai Shang Xian Guan (海山仙馆), which was renowned throughout China for its magnificent collection of stone inscriptions and rare books; Li Xiang Yuan (荔香园); and Ting Xue Peng (听雪蓬). These gardens represented the pinnacle of Lingnan garden design, a tradition that emphasized the harmonious integration of architecture and nature, and the creation of intimate, tranquil spaces that encouraged contemplation.

The poet and official Zhang Weiping, a native of Guangzhou, celebrated the beauty of Litchi Bay in his famous poem "Recalling the Floral Scene of Yangcheng," which contains the immortal line: "With green water and red lychees, the Liwan district shines like a painting." This poetic vision of the area is one that visitors can still feel today as they walk along the willow-lined shores of Liwan Lake.

### From Bay to Park: The 1958 Transformation

The 20th century brought dramatic changes to Litchi Bay. Siltation had already transformed much of the bay into marshland over the preceding centuries. In 1958, faced with a growing urban population and a need to improve living conditions, the Guangzhou Municipal Government launched a massive public works project.

Under the leadership of Mayor Zhu Guang, a remarkable thing happened: hundreds of thousands of ordinary Guangzhou citizens volunteered their labor to excavate the lakes that would become Liwan Lake Park. This was not a project imposed from above but a genuine communal effort, with students, factory workers, and neighborhood residents working together to dig the lake beds and construct the banks. The project took more than a year and was completed in 1959. In 1960, the park was officially named "Liwan Lake Park," and the characters of its name were written by Shen Junru, a former Chief Justice of the Supreme Court of China.

The creation of Liwan Lake Park was not just an infrastructure project; it was an act of civic imagination. The people of Guangzhou reclaimed a piece of their ancient heritage, transforming what had become a degraded waterway into a beautiful public space that honored the legacy of Litchi Bay while creating something entirely new.

### The Park Today: A Living Legacy

Today, Liwan Lake Park comprises four interconnected lakes: Xiaocui Lake (小翠湖), Yucui Lake (玉翠湖), Ruyi Lake (如意湖), and Wuxiu Lake (五秀湖). These lakes, covering 62% of the park's total area, are connected by graceful arched bridges and adorned with traditional pavilions that echo the grand gardens of the Qing Dynasty.

Perhaps the most poignant link to the past is the Haishan Xian Pavilion (海山仙馆), a beautiful structure that pays tribute to the fabled garden that once stood on the same site. Standing on the pavilion's curved bridge, with lotus blossoms swaying in the breeze and the sound of Cantonese opera drifting from the nearby stage, you can feel the layers of history beneath your feet.

The ancient lychee trees that gave the bay its name may be gone, but their spirit remains. In every corner of Liwan Lake Park, from the tea houses where locals gather for morning conversation to the free Cantonese opera performances that echo the Ming Dynasty's "Fisherman's Song," the past is not just remembered but lived.

### Conclusion

The history of Liwan Lake Park is a story of transformation and continuity. From a natural bay teeming with lychee trees, to a celebrated garden retreat for the Qing elite, to a public park built by the hands of ordinary citizens, each era has left its mark. When you visit Liwan Lake Park, you are not just visiting a park; you are stepping into Guangzhou's living memory.

### Introduction

Every afternoon, beneath the ancient banyan trees of Liwan Lake Park, the unmistakable sound of Cantonese opera fills the air. And remarkably, it is entirely free.

The Cantonese opera performances at Liwan Lake Park are one of Guangzhou's best-kept secrets, a living tradition that connects the city's present to its cultural past. For visitors, attending a performance is not just entertainment; it is an immersion into the heart of Cantonese culture.

### The Lychee Bay Grand Theater

The performances take place at the Lychee Bay Grand Theater (荔枝湾大戏台), an open-air stage located within the park. This is not a formal theater with ticketed seats and velvet curtains; it is a simple stage beneath the trees, where audiences sit on benches or stand in the shade, coming and going as they please.

The theater has become a beloved institution in the Liwan community. According to local reports, performances are held every afternoon from 2:15 PM to 4:45 PM, year-round without a break. This remarkable consistency is a testament to the dedication of the performers, many of whom are elderly amateurs who have been singing Cantonese opera for decades.

### What to Expect

**The Performances**

The repertoire typically includes classic Cantonese opera pieces, known for their elaborate costumes, stylized gestures, and distinctive vocal techniques. Even if you do not understand Cantonese, the visual spectacle alone is captivating. The performers' movements are highly choreographed, with every gesture and expression conveying meaning.

The music is provided by a small ensemble of traditional instruments, including the erhu (a two-stringed bowed instrument), the yangqin (a hammered dulcimer), and various percussion instruments. The sound is distinctive and powerful, filling the park and drawing passersby to stop and listen.

**The Audience**

The audience is as much a part of the experience as the performance. You will see elderly locals who have been attending these performances for years, nodding along to familiar melodies. There are also younger visitors, curious tourists, and families with children. The atmosphere is relaxed and communal, with people chatting quietly, drinking tea, and enjoying the performance at their own pace.

### The Cultural Significance of Cantonese Opera

Cantonese opera, known as Jyut Kek (粵劇) in Cantonese, is one of the major forms of Chinese opera and was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2009. Its roots go back centuries, and it remains one of the most beloved traditional art forms in Guangdong province, Hong Kong, and Macau.

The art form is characterized by its distinctive singing style, which blends high-pitched falsetto with natural voice, and its elaborate costumes and makeup. The stories performed are typically drawn from Chinese history, legends, and folklore, with themes of loyalty, love, and moral virtue.

The free performances at Liwan Lake Park play a crucial role in keeping this tradition alive. They provide a platform for amateur performers to practice their craft and for audiences to experience the art form in an accessible, informal setting. For many older residents of Liwan, these performances are a cherished part of their daily routine.

### Practical Tips for Attending

**What to Bring:** A hat or umbrella for sun protection, a bottle of water, and perhaps a small cushion if you plan to sit on the benches. Many locals bring their own tea in thermos flasks.

**Etiquette:** There is no formal etiquette, but it is polite to remain quiet during the performance and to avoid blocking the view of others. Photography is generally allowed, but flash should be avoided.

**Combine with Other Activities:** The opera performances are a perfect complement to a morning of walking around the park. Plan to arrive around 1:00 PM, take a stroll around the lakes, have lunch at one of the nearby restaurants, and then settle in for the performance at 2:15 PM.

### Beyond the Main Stage

In addition to the organized performances at the Lychee Bay Grand Theater, you may encounter impromptu Cantonese opera singing in other parts of the park, particularly in the mornings. Groups of retirees often gather to sing together, accompanied by portable amplifiers or simple instruments. These informal sessions are equally authentic and offer a more intimate glimpse into the living tradition of Cantonese opera.

### Conclusion

Attending a Cantonese opera performance at Liwan Lake Park is one of the most authentic cultural experiences Guangzhou has to offer. It is not a polished tourist show; it is a genuine expression of community and tradition. Whether you are a devoted fan of Chinese opera or a curious traveler, the free performances at Liwan Lake Park will leave you with a deeper appreciation for the living culture of Guangzhou.

### Introduction

Every summer, Liwan Lake Park undergoes a spectacular transformation. The calm waters of its four lakes become carpeted with lotus flowers, their pink and white blossoms rising above the broad green leaves like jewels set in jade. This annual event, known as lotus season, draws photographers, nature lovers, and locals to the park in droves.

For anyone visiting Guangzhou between June and August, the lotus blooms at Liwan Lake Park are an unmissable spectacle. This guide will tell you everything you need to know about when to visit, where to go, and how to capture the perfect lotus photograph.

### When to Visit: The Lotus Blooming Calendar

The lotus season at Liwan Lake Park follows a predictable pattern, though exact timing can vary slightly from year to year depending on weather conditions.

**Late May to Early June: The First Buds**

The lotus season begins in late May or early June, when the first buds begin to emerge from the water. This is a period of anticipation, as the buds grow taller and begin to show hints of color. The leaves are fresh and green, and the ponds have a delicate, promising atmosphere.

**Mid-June to July: Peak Bloom**

The peak blooming period is generally from mid-June through July. During this time, the lotus flowers are in full glory, with large, vibrant blossoms covering much of the water surface. This is the best time for photography and for experiencing the full beauty of the lotus season.

**August to September: Late Season**

By August, the lotus flowers begin to decline, though some blossoms may still be visible. The leaves may start to yellow, and the seed pods become prominent. For those who appreciate the quieter, more contemplative aspects of nature, this late-season period has its own charm.

### Best Times of Day

**Early Morning (7:00 AM – 11:00 AM)**

Morning is the best time to see lotus flowers at their freshest. The flowers are fully open, the light is soft and golden, and the park is relatively quiet. According to photography experts, the morning light is ideal for capturing the delicate textures and colors of the lotus blooms, which are often still dotted with morning dew.

**Late Afternoon (4:00 PM – 6:00 PM)**

The late afternoon is another excellent time for lotus viewing. The light is warm and the shadows are longer, creating a more dramatic and atmospheric scene. The lotus flowers may begin to close slightly, but the soft light creates beautiful photographic opportunities.

**Avoid Midday (11:00 AM – 3:00 PM)**

Midday is generally the least ideal time for lotus viewing. The light is harsh and flat, and the flowers may be fully closed or wilting in the heat. Additionally, this is when the park is most crowded.

### Best Viewing Spots

**Haishan Xian Pavilion (海山仙馆)**

The area around Haishan Xian Pavilion is widely considered the best spot for lotus viewing in the park. The pavilion, with its traditional architecture and curved bridge, provides a stunning backdrop for the lotus ponds. The combination of pink blossoms, green leaves, and classic Lingnan architecture creates a scene of unparalleled beauty.

**North Gate Lotus Pond**

The lotus pond near the North Gate is another excellent viewing area. As soon as you enter the park from this gate, you will see a large lotus pond spreading out before you. This area is particularly good for close-up photography of individual flowers.

**Wuxiu Lake (五秀湖)**

Wuxiu Lake is the quietest of the four lakes and offers a more peaceful lotus viewing experience. The lotus flowers here are less densely packed, but the scenery is beautiful and the crowds are thinner. This is a good choice for photographers who want to avoid the busiest areas.

### Lotus Photography Tips

**Use a Telephoto Lens:** A telephoto lens (100mm or longer) allows you to isolate individual flowers and compress the background, creating beautiful, intimate portraits of the lotus blooms.

**Shoot Wide for Landscapes:** A wide-angle lens is useful for capturing the full scale of the lotus ponds, especially when combined with the traditional architecture of the park's pavilions and bridges.

**Embrace Backlighting:** Lotus flowers are particularly beautiful when backlit by the morning or late afternoon sun. The light shines through the translucent petals, creating a glowing effect.

**Include Architecture:** The lotus flowers are most photogenic when framed against the park's traditional buildings. Look for compositions that include pavilions, bridges, or the curved eaves of Lingnan architecture.

**Respect the Plants:** Do not pick lotus flowers or leaves, and avoid stepping into the ponds. The lotus plants are fragile, and damaging them harms the ecosystem.

### Beyond Liwan Lake: Other Lotus Viewing Spots in Guangzhou

While Liwan Lake Park is one of the best places to see lotus flowers in Guangzhou, it is not the only option. Other notable lotus viewing spots include Liuhua Lake Park (流花湖公园) and Liurong Temple (六榕寺). Each has its own character and advantages. Lotus season at Liwan Lake Park is a highlight of Guangzhou's summer calendar. Whether you are a photographer seeking the perfect shot or a traveler looking to connect with the natural beauty of Guangzhou, the lotus blooms at Liwan Lake Park will not disappoint.

## Article 10: Food Near Liwan Lake Park: A Xiguan Culinary Guide

**Target Keywords:** food near Liwan Lake Park, Xiguan food guide, Liwan District restaurants, Guangzhou street food Xiguan

### Introduction

The old Xiguan district of Guangzhou is one of the city's premier culinary destinations. Here, in the narrow streets and bustling markets around Liwan Lake Park, you will find the flavors that define Cantonese cuisine: delicate dim sum, fragrant rice noodle rolls, savory wonton noodles, and sweet double-skin milk.

A visit to Liwan Lake Park is incomplete without exploring the surrounding food scene. This guide will introduce you to the best dishes, the best places to eat them, and the culinary traditions that make Xiguan one of the most delicious neighborhoods in China.

### The Flavors of Xiguan

Xiguan cuisine is the foundation of Cantonese cooking. It is characterized by freshness, subtlety, and a respect for natural flavors. The cooking techniques are simple but precise: steaming, poaching, stir-frying, and roasting. The ingredients are local and seasonal: fresh seafood from the Pearl River, vegetables from the surrounding farms, and rice in all its forms.

### Essential Dishes to Try

**Wonton Noodles (云吞面)**

Wonton noodles are a Guangzhou institution. The dish consists of thin egg noodles, plump wontons filled with shrimp and pork, and a clear broth made from dried flounder and pork bones. The noodles should be springy, the wontons should be delicate, and the broth should be light but flavorful.

Where to try: Look for traditional noodle shops in the streets around Liwan Lake Park. The best shops are often small and unassuming, with a few tables and a loyal local following.

**Rice Noodle Rolls (肠粉)**

Rice noodle rolls, known as cheung fun in Cantonese, are a popular breakfast and snack food. They are made by steaming a thin layer of rice batter, filling it with shrimp, pork, or vegetables, and rolling it into a cylinder. The rolls are served with light soy sauce and often accompanied by a drizzle of sesame oil.

Where to try: Street vendors and small restaurants throughout Xiguan serve excellent rice noodle rolls. Look for the steamers, which are a sign of freshly made rolls.

**Shrimp Dumplings (虾饺)**

Shrimp dumplings, or har gow, are the crown jewel of Cantonese dim sum. They are made with a translucent wheat starch wrapper and filled with whole shrimp, bamboo shoots, and a touch of pork fat. The best har gow have a wrapper that is thin and slightly chewy, and a filling that is juicy and flavorful.

Where to try: Dim sum restaurants throughout the area serve har gow, but the best are found in the traditional tea houses near Liwan Lake Park.

**Barbecue Pork Buns (叉烧包)**

Barbecue pork buns, known as cha siu bao, are a dim sum classic. They are made with a soft, fluffy dough and filled with sweet, savory barbecue pork. The buns can be steamed or baked, and each has its own distinct texture and flavor.

Where to try: The tea houses and dim sum restaurants around Liwan Lake Park serve excellent cha siu bao. They are a must-try for any visitor to Guangzhou.

**Double-Skin Milk (双皮奶)**

Double-skin milk is a traditional Cantonese dessert made from buffalo milk, egg whites, and sugar. The name comes from the thin layer of skin that forms on the surface of the milk as it cools. The dessert is smooth, creamy, and subtly sweet.

Where to try: Dessert shops throughout Xiguan serve double-skin milk. It is a perfect way to end a meal or to refresh yourself after a long walk.

**Litchi and Other Local Fruits**

The Liwan district is the historic home of Litchi Bay, and lychees remain an important part of the local food culture. During the summer months, you will find fresh lychees for sale in the markets and fruit stalls around the park. Other local fruits to look for include longan, wampee, and star fruit.

### Where to Eat

**Tea Houses (茶楼)**

The tea houses of Guangzhou are the traditional venues for dim sum. They are bustling, noisy, and full of life. The best tea houses near Liwan Lake Park serve dim sum from early morning until lunchtime, and many stay open into the afternoon.

**Street Food Stalls**

The streets around Liwan Lake Park are lined with food stalls selling everything from rice noodle rolls to fried snacks. Street food is an essential part of the Xiguan culinary experience, and it is affordable and delicious.

**Restaurants in Yongqingfang**

The revitalized Yongqingfang neighborhood has a growing number of restaurants and cafes, ranging from traditional Cantonese to modern fusion. This is a good option for a sit-down meal with a more contemporary atmosphere.

**Pantang Food Street**

Pantang Food Street (泮塘美食街), located near Liwan Lake Park, is a popular destination for local food lovers. The street is lined with small restaurants and stalls serving traditional Xiguan dishes, and it is particularly lively in the evenings.

### Practical Tips

**Eat Where the Locals Eat:** The best food is often found in the smallest, least assuming restaurants. Look for places with a queue of locals.

**Try Everything:** Do not be afraid to try new dishes. Cantonese cuisine is diverse and delicious, and you may discover a new favorite.

**Drink Tea:** Tea is an essential part of the Cantonese dining experience. It aids digestion and enhances the flavors of the food.

**Be Adventurous:** Some of the best dishes in Xiguan are the ones you have never heard of. Be open to trying new things.

### Conclusion

The food around Liwan Lake Park is one of the great pleasures of visiting Guangzhou. From the delicate flavors of dim sum to the robust taste of wonton noodles, the cuisine of Xiguan is a reflection of the district's history, culture, and people. By exploring the food scene around the park, you will not only enjoy delicious meals but also gain a deeper understanding of what makes Guangzhou one of the world's great culinary cities.
