Quick answer

The area around Liwan Lake Park is Xiguan — the birthplace of Cantonese snack culture. Within a 5–20 minute walk you can have morning dim sum (饮茶), wonton noodles, rice-noodle rolls (肠粉), double-skin milk (双皮奶) and boat congee. Go before 10 am for the full local tea-house atmosphere.

Where to look (by area, not hype)

Streets around the park's east entrances

The lanes toward Longjin West Road hold old tea houses and noodle shops that open at dawn for the post-tai-chi crowd. Look for places full of retired regulars reading newspapers — that is your quality signal.

Shangxiajiu Pedestrian Street area (10–20 min walk)

The arcade streets around Shangxiajiu (上下九) concentrate classic Cantonese snack institutions and dessert shops. Side streets off the main pedestrian drag are cheaper and calmer.

Enning Road / Yongqingfang (10–20 min walk)

Restored heritage lanes mixing old dessert shops with newer cafes — the best choice if you want coffee after a traditional morning.

How a Xiguan morning works

  1. 7:30 — arrive at the park for tai chi and opera atmosphere.
  2. 9:00 — walk out the east entrances to a tea house.
  3. 9:15 — order tea (pu'er or chrysanthemum) and 3–4 dim sum baskets.
  4. 10:30 — stroll toward Shangxiajiu for dessert: double-skin milk.
  5. 11:30 — wonton noodles or rice-noodle rolls if you're still standing.

Practical tips

  • Pay with WeChat Pay or Alipay — set these up before arriving in China; small shops rarely take cards.
  • Tea houses charge a small per-person tea fee (茶位费) — this is normal.
  • Peak dim sum is 8–10 am; the best baskets can sell out by 11.
  • Menus in this area are often Chinese-only; photograph dishes you fancy on other tables and point.
  • Famous old-name restaurants (老字号) have queues at weekends — weekday mornings are painless.
