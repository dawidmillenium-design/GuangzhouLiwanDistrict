Quick answer

Liwan Lake Park, also called Liwanhu Park (荔湾湖公园), is a public lake park in Guangzhou's Liwan District. It is known for its peaceful lakes, traditional pavilions, local leisure activities, Cantonese opera culture, and proximity to old Guangzhou neighborhoods such as Shangxiajiu and Yongqingfang.

What is Liwan Lake Park?

Liwan Lake Park is a lake-centered public park in the heart of Xiguan (西关), the historic western quarter of Guangzhou. Created in 1958 around a chain of connected lakes — historically linked to the area's old waterways and lychee orchards that gave Liwan ("lychee bay") its name — the park blends Lingnan garden design with everyday Cantonese neighborhood life.

Unlike Guangzhou's grand landmark parks, Liwanhu is where the city breathes: retired opera singers gather under pavilions at dawn, chess players argue over boards in the shade, and families rent pedal boats on weekends. For travelers, it is the single easiest place to watch old Guangzhou culture happening rather than staged.

Why visit Liwan Lake Park?

  • See local daily life away from tourist crowds.
  • Photograph traditional pavilions, bridges and lake reflections.
  • Hear informal Cantonese opera and community music.
  • Combine easily with a Shangxiajiu food walk and Yongqingfang heritage lanes.
  • Easy access from central Guangzhou by metro or a short walk from the old town.
  • Ideal for a relaxed morning or a sunset visit.

Location and map

The park sits in Liwan District, west of Guangzhou's historic center, bordered by old-town streets and close to the former foreign concession area of Shamian across the canal district. The main entrances lie along Longjin West Road (龙津西路) and the Fengyuan/Yuehui area by Liwan Lake itself.

Search 荔湾湖公园 in Amap (Gaode), Baidu Maps or Apple Maps for live entrances; Google Maps works but is less precise inside mainland China.

Opening hours and ticket information

Quick answer: Liwan Lake Park is a public city park that has generally been free to enter, with the grounds open from early morning into the evening. Hours and any fees for specific activities (such as boat rental) can change — check on-site notices or recent visitor reports before you go.

  • Grounds: open from early morning (locals arrive before sunrise for exercise) until evening lighting hours.
  • Entry fee: the park has historically been free; paid activities are separate.
  • Boats: pedal boats are typically rented by the half hour at the lake piers; bring mobile payment (WeChat Pay / Alipay) or small cash.
  • Holidays: expect heavy crowds during Golden Week (early October) and Lunar New Year flower-fair periods.

Best time to visit

By time of day

  • Early morning (6–9 am): tai chi groups, opera singers warming up, soft mist on the lake, few tourists.
  • Late afternoon (4–6 pm): golden-hour light on pavilions and bridges — the photographer's window.
  • Evening: cooler air, lantern glow and families strolling; a relaxed pairing with dinner in Xiguan.

By season

  • Spring (Mar–Apr): fresh greenery, mild weather, occasional drizzle.
  • Summer (May–Sep): lotus in bloom on the lake, but heat, humidity and sudden downpours; typhoon season peaks Jul–Sep.
  • Autumn (Oct–Nov): the most comfortable walking weather of the year.
  • Winter (Dec–Feb): soft light, fewer crowds, cool but rarely cold.

Weather warnings: Guangzhou is humid year-round. Carry water, mosquito repellent (essential near the lake at dusk) and a compact umbrella.

What to see and do

  • Lake loop walk — a flat, shaded circuit around the connected lakes; about an hour at strolling pace.
  • Pavilions and bridges — Lingnan-style rooflines, moon bridges and covered corridors; best light near sunset.
  • Pedal boats — rent at the lake piers on weekends for a water-level view of the pavilions. Prices and boat types: boat rental guide.
  • Cantonese opera corners (粤曲) — informal morning sessions where local singers perform with erhu and percussion accompaniment; listen respectfully from the edge of the circle. Daily afternoon shows: free Cantonese opera guide.
  • Chess and cards — shaded tables host all-day xiangqi (Chinese chess) matches.
  • Morning exercise — tai chi, fan dancing, sword routines and badminton on the plazas.
  • Lotus beds — peak bloom roughly June to August on the lake's quieter arms. Timing and spots: lotus season guide.
  • Tea nearby — the surrounding Xiguan streets are famous for morning dim sum (饮茶) culture.

Photo spots

  • Pavilion reflections on calm mornings — shoot low from the water's edge.
  • Moon bridges framed through covered corridors.
  • Lotus close-ups in June–August, ideally before 9 am.
  • Golden hour on the west arm of the lake for backlit rooflines.
  • Rainy-day mood: wet stone, umbrellas, opera singers under shelter.
  • Local-life portraits — always ask with a smile and gesture before shooting close.

Visitor tips

  • Bring water — humid heat builds fast even in spring.
  • Use mosquito repellent, especially near the lake at dusk.
  • Wear comfortable flat shoes; paths are easy but you'll walk more than planned.
  • Respect opera and music circles — listen first, photograph later, quietly.
  • Ask before close portraits; a smile and showing the photo afterwards goes far.
  • Set up WeChat Pay or Alipay before arrival; small vendors rarely take cards.
  • Download an offline map (Amap works best in Guangzhou).
  • Pair the visit with Shangxiajiu — they belong to the same walk.

Accessibility and family info

  • Paths: mostly flat paved loops suitable for strollers; some bridges have steps.
  • Shade: good tree and pavilion cover; midday summer sun is still intense on open stretches.
  • Restrooms: public toilets are located near main entrances and plazas; carry tissues and hand sanitizer.
  • Seating: abundant benches and pavilion seating — genuinely senior-friendly.
  • Children: watch kids near unfenced lake edges; boat piers require life jackets for children.
  • Wheelchairs: the main lakeside loop is manageable; detour around stepped bridges.

Nearby attractions

  • Shangxiajiu Pedestrian Street (上下九) — Qilou arcade shopping and snack street, 10–20 min walk.
  • Yongqingfang (永庆坊) — restored heritage lanes with cafes, craft shops and the Cantonese Opera Art Museum nearby.
  • Chen Clan Ancestral Hall (陈家祠) — Guangdong's masterwork of folk carving; one metro stop or a short taxi away.
  • Shamian Island (沙面) — colonial-era riverside island, 20–30 min by foot or short ride.
  • Xiguan food streets — see best food near Liwan Lake Park.

Suggested itineraries

2-hour quick visit

  1. Enter from Longjin West Road.
  2. Walk the lake loop counter-clockwise.
  3. Stop at the main pavilion for reflection photos.
  4. Pause at the opera corner if singing is underway.
  5. Exit south toward Shangxiajiu for snacks.

Half-day old Guangzhou route

  1. 8:00 — Liwan Lake Park at local-life peak.
  2. 10:00 — dim sum or wonton noodles in Xiguan.
  3. 11:30 — Shangxiajiu Pedestrian Street arcades.
  4. 14:00 — Yongqingfang heritage lanes and opera museum.
  5. 17:30 — return to the lake for sunset reflections.

Photography route

  1. Arrive 60 minutes before sunset.
  2. Lake reflections on the west arm.
  3. Pavilion details and framed bridge shots.
  4. Local-life candids with permission.
  5. Blue hour: lantern and corridor lights.

FAQ: Liwan Lake Park visitor questions

Where is Liwan Lake Park?

In Liwan District, Guangzhou, Guangdong, China — in the historic Xiguan quarter, near Shangxiajiu and Yongqingfang.

Is Liwan Lake Park free?

It is a public park that has generally been free to enter. Activities like boat rental cost extra. Verify current policy on arrival.

What is the best time to visit?

Early morning for local culture; late afternoon for photography; October–November for weather; June–August for lotus.

How do I get there by metro?

Changshou Lu (Line 1) or Ruyifang (Line 6) stations, then a 10–20 minute walk or short taxi. See our transport guide.

Can you rent boats?

Yes — pedal boats are typically available at lake piers, especially on weekends. Prices are posted at the pier.

Is it good for photography?

Excellent — pavilion reflections, moon bridges, lotus beds and candid local life. Golden hour and post-rain light are the highlights.

Is it suitable for kids?

Yes: flat paths, boats and open plazas. Supervise children near unfenced water edges.

Is it accessible for seniors?

Very — flat main loop, abundant seating and shade make it one of Guangzhou's most senior-friendly parks.

Liwan Lake Park vs Yuexiu Park?

Liwanhu is smaller, local and culture-focused; Yuexiu is larger with major monuments. Full comparison: Liwan Lake Park vs Yuexiu Park — and the extended version with itinerary advice: which Guangzhou park should you visit?.

Can you visit at night?

The park stays open into the evening with lighting along main paths; it is calm and pleasant after dinner, though less scenic than golden hour.

Are there restrooms?

Yes, near main entrances and plazas. Bring tissues and sanitizer as a precaution.

What should I wear?

Light breathable clothing, comfortable flat shoes, hat in summer; carry an umbrella year-round.

Is the park crowded?

Weekday mornings are calm; weekends and public holidays (especially Golden Week) are busy but still feel local.

Can you see Cantonese opera there?

Yes — informal morning sessions by local singers are a park institution. They are community gatherings, not ticketed shows.

How much time do I need?

1–3 hours for the park itself; half a day combined with Shangxiajiu and Yongqingfang.

What food is nearby?

Xiguan is Guangzhou's snack heartland — dim sum, wonton noodles, double-skin milk, rice-noodle rolls. See our food guide.

Is it worth visiting in rain?

Yes — light rain empties the paths and the lake turns atmospheric. Avoid visits during typhoon warnings.

Are mosquitoes a problem?

They can be near the water at dusk in warm months — repellent solves it.

Can I combine it with Shangxiajiu?

Absolutely — they are a 10–20 minute walk apart and form the classic old-Guangzhou pairing.

Is Liwan Lake Park worth visiting for tourists?

If you want everyday Guangzhou culture rather than landmark sightseeing, it is one of the city's most rewarding stops.

Final verdict

Liwan Lake Park is best for travelers who want to see everyday Guangzhou, not just landmark tourist spots. It is especially valuable if you enjoy photography, local culture, old neighborhoods and relaxed walking routes. Go in the morning, stay for the singing, and walk out hungry into Xiguan.
