Quick answer

Rain is not a reason to skip Liwan Lake Park — many say it is at its most atmospheric during a gentle shower. Walk the covered bridges, shelter in the traditional pavilions and tea houses, and photograph the mist rising over the lotus ponds. Bring an umbrella, a rain jacket, and waterproof footwear for slippery paths.

Introduction

Guangzhou's rainy season, which typically runs from April to September, can put a damper on outdoor plans. But at Liwan Lake Park, rain does not mean retreat. In fact, many locals and experienced travelers will tell you that the park is at its most beautiful and atmospheric during a gentle rain. The mist rising from the lakes, the sound of raindrops on lotus leaves, and the glistening wet stones of ancient bridges create a scene of haunting beauty that fair-weather visitors never see.

This guide will help you make the most of a rainy day at Liwan Lake Park, with suggestions for what to see, where to shelter, and how to stay dry while still enjoying the park's unique atmosphere.

The Magic of Liwan Lake in the Rain

There is a reason why Chinese landscape painters have long been drawn to misty, rain-soaked scenes. The rain transforms Liwan Lake Park into a living ink painting. The lines of the bridges soften, the colors of the lotus flowers intensify, and the distant pavilions fade into a gray-blue haze.

The local saying that the park is a "huge energy field" during the rainy season is not just poetic; it reflects a genuine appreciation for the way rain enhances the park's tranquility. The crowds thin out, the noise of the city fades, and you are left with the elemental experience of water, stone, and greenery.

Best Rainy Day Activities

Walking the Covered Bridges

Liwan Lake Park is fortunate to have several covered bridges and walkways that allow visitors to move through the park while staying largely protected from the rain. These traditional structures, with their wooden roofs and lattice screens, are both practical and beautiful. Walking through them during a rainstorm, pausing to watch the water cascade off the eaves, is a quintessential Chinese garden experience.

Sheltering in Pavilions

The park's many pavilions are perfect rainy day retreats. These open-sided structures, built in the traditional Lingnan style, offer shelter while still allowing you to enjoy the view. Bring a thermos of tea and a good book, and you can spend a peaceful afternoon watching the rain fall over the lotus ponds.

Visiting the Tea House

The park's tea houses are ideal places to wait out a shower. Order a pot of tea and some dim sum, and let the rain create a soothing background soundtrack to your meal. The tea houses are often filled with locals who have retreated from the rain, creating a cozy and convivial atmosphere.

Exploring Haishan Xian Pavilion

The Haishan Xian Pavilion area is particularly atmospheric in the rain. The pavilion itself provides shelter, and the views over the lotus pond are stunning. The mist rising from the water and the raindrops on the lotus leaves create a scene that feels almost otherworldly.

What to Bring

Umbrella: A sturdy umbrella is essential. Compact umbrellas are convenient, but a larger umbrella offers better protection.

Rain Jacket: A lightweight, waterproof jacket is useful for walking between sheltered areas. It leaves your hands free and provides better coverage than an umbrella in windy conditions.

Waterproof Footwear: The park's paths can become slippery when wet. Shoes with good grip are important for safety.

Plastic Bags: Bring a few plastic bags to protect your phone, camera, and other valuables from the rain.

Thermos: A thermos of hot tea will keep you warm and comfortable while you wait out the rain.

Rainy Day Photography

For photographers, a rainy day at Liwan Lake Park offers unique opportunities. The soft, diffused light eliminates harsh shadows and brings out the rich colors of the vegetation. The reflections on the wet stones and the mist rising from the water create images of great mood and atmosphere.

Tips for Rainy Day Photography:

  • Protect Your Gear: Use a rain cover for your camera or a plastic bag with a hole for the lens. Wipe the lens frequently with a microfiber cloth.
  • Embrace Reflections: Wet surfaces create beautiful reflections. Look for compositions that incorporate the reflections of bridges, pavilions, and trees in the wet stone or water.
  • Capture Motion: A slow shutter speed can capture the motion of raindrops falling into the ponds, creating a soft, dreamy effect.
  • Focus on Details: Raindrops on lotus leaves, water dripping from eaves, wet stone textures — the details are where the beauty of a rainy day lies.

Safety Considerations

Slippery Paths: The park's stone paths can become very slippery when wet. Walk slowly and carefully, especially on bridges and near the water's edge.

Lightning: If there is thunder and lightning, seek shelter immediately. Avoid standing under tall trees or in open areas.

Flooding: During heavy rain, some low-lying areas of the park may flood. Stay on higher ground and avoid walking through standing water.

Nearby Indoor Alternatives

If the rain becomes too heavy, there are several indoor attractions near Liwan Lake Park that are worth visiting:

  • Cantonese Opera Art Museum: Located near Yongqingfang, this museum offers a fascinating introduction to Cantonese opera, with costumes, instruments, and historical exhibits.
  • Liwan Museum: Housed in a beautifully restored Xiguan mansion, the Liwan Museum tells the story of the district's history and culture.
  • Yongqingfang: This historic neighborhood has many covered walkways, shops, and cafes where you can wait out the rain.

Conclusion

A rainy day at Liwan Lake Park is not a setback; it is an opportunity. The rain reveals a side of the park that fair-weather visitors never see: a world of mist, reflection, and quiet beauty. With the right preparation and the right attitude, a rainy day at Liwan Lake Park can be one of the most memorable experiences of your trip to Guangzhou.
