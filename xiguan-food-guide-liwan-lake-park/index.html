Quick answer

The best food near Liwan Lake Park is classic Xiguan Cantonese cuisine: wonton noodles (云吞面), rice noodle rolls (肠粉), shrimp dumplings (虾饺), barbecue pork buns (叉烧包) and double-skin milk (双皮奶). Eat at traditional tea houses for dim sum, street stalls and Pantang Food Street for local dishes, or Yongqingfang for a more contemporary meal. The golden rule: eat where the locals eat — small, unassuming shops with a queue.

Introduction

The old Xiguan district of Guangzhou is one of the city's premier culinary destinations. Here, in the narrow streets and bustling markets around Liwan Lake Park, you will find the flavors that define Cantonese cuisine: delicate dim sum, fragrant rice noodle rolls, savory wonton noodles, and sweet double-skin milk.

A visit to Liwan Lake Park is incomplete without exploring the surrounding food scene. This guide will introduce you to the best dishes, the best places to eat them, and the culinary traditions that make Xiguan one of the most delicious neighborhoods in China.

The Flavors of Xiguan

Xiguan cuisine is the foundation of Cantonese cooking. It is characterized by freshness, subtlety, and a respect for natural flavors. The cooking techniques are simple but precise: steaming, poaching, stir-frying, and roasting. The ingredients are local and seasonal: fresh seafood from the Pearl River, vegetables from the surrounding farms, and rice in all its forms.

Essential Dishes to Try

Wonton Noodles (云吞面)

Wonton noodles are a Guangzhou institution. The dish consists of thin egg noodles, plump wontons filled with shrimp and pork, and a clear broth made from dried flounder and pork bones. The noodles should be springy, the wontons should be delicate, and the broth should be light but flavorful.

Where to try: Look for traditional noodle shops in the streets around Liwan Lake Park. The best shops are often small and unassuming, with a few tables and a loyal local following.

Rice Noodle Rolls (肠粉)

Rice noodle rolls, known as cheung fun in Cantonese, are a popular breakfast and snack food. They are made by steaming a thin layer of rice batter, filling it with shrimp, pork, or vegetables, and rolling it into a cylinder. The rolls are served with light soy sauce and often accompanied by a drizzle of sesame oil.

Where to try: Street vendors and small restaurants throughout Xiguan serve excellent rice noodle rolls. Look for the steamers, which are a sign of freshly made rolls.

Shrimp Dumplings (虾饺)

Shrimp dumplings, or har gow, are the crown jewel of Cantonese dim sum. They are made with a translucent wheat starch wrapper and filled with whole shrimp, bamboo shoots, and a touch of pork fat. The best har gow have a wrapper that is thin and slightly chewy, and a filling that is juicy and flavorful.

Where to try: Dim sum restaurants throughout the area serve har gow, but the best are found in the traditional tea houses near Liwan Lake Park.

Barbecue Pork Buns (叉烧包)

Barbecue pork buns, known as cha siu bao, are a dim sum classic. They are made with a soft, fluffy dough and filled with sweet, savory barbecue pork. The buns can be steamed or baked, and each has its own distinct texture and flavor.

Where to try: The tea houses and dim sum restaurants around Liwan Lake Park serve excellent cha siu bao. They are a must-try for any visitor to Guangzhou.

Double-Skin Milk (双皮奶)

Double-skin milk is a traditional Cantonese dessert made from buffalo milk, egg whites, and sugar. The name comes from the thin layer of skin that forms on the surface of the milk as it cools. The dessert is smooth, creamy, and subtly sweet.

Where to try: Dessert shops throughout Xiguan serve double-skin milk. It is a perfect way to end a meal or to refresh yourself after a long walk.

Litchi and Other Local Fruits

The Liwan district is the historic home of Litchi Bay, and lychees remain an important part of the local food culture. During the summer months, you will find fresh lychees for sale in the markets and fruit stalls around the park. Other local fruits to look for include longan, wampee, and star fruit.

Where to Eat

Tea Houses (茶楼)

The tea houses of Guangzhou are the traditional venues for dim sum. They are bustling, noisy, and full of life. The best tea houses near Liwan Lake Park serve dim sum from early morning until lunchtime, and many stay open into the afternoon.

Street Food Stalls

The streets around Liwan Lake Park are lined with food stalls selling everything from rice noodle rolls to fried snacks. Street food is an essential part of the Xiguan culinary experience, and it is affordable and delicious.

Restaurants in Yongqingfang

The revitalized Yongqingfang neighborhood has a growing number of restaurants and cafes, ranging from traditional Cantonese to modern fusion. This is a good option for a sit-down meal with a more contemporary atmosphere.

Pantang Food Street

Pantang Food Street (泮塘美食街), located near Liwan Lake Park, is a popular destination for local food lovers. The street is lined with small restaurants and stalls serving traditional Xiguan dishes, and it is particularly lively in the evenings.

Practical Tips

  • Eat Where the Locals Eat: The best food is often found in the smallest, least assuming restaurants. Look for places with a queue of locals.
  • Try Everything: Do not be afraid to try new dishes. Cantonese cuisine is diverse and delicious, and you may discover a new favorite.
  • Drink Tea: Tea is an essential part of the Cantonese dining experience. It aids digestion and enhances the flavors of the food.
  • Be Adventurous: Some of the best dishes in Xiguan are the ones you have never heard of. Be open to trying new things.

Conclusion

The food around Liwan Lake Park is one of the great pleasures of visiting Guangzhou. From the delicate flavors of dim sum to the robust taste of wonton noodles, the cuisine of Xiguan is a reflection of the district's history, culture, and people. By exploring the food scene around the park, you will not only enjoy delicious meals but also gain a deeper understanding of what makes Guangzhou one of the world's great culinary cities.
